Reject or disclose an incompatible fit instead of modifying source measurements to match a template.
3. Fit chest, abdomen, pelvis, upper/lower arms and thighs/calves as linked sections with separately variable breadth and depth. Acceptance tolerances must follow source precision and fitting uncertainty; record errors in physical units. Keep camera scale and pose separate from anatomy. Compare front, profile and neutral unpadded bodies at common scale, then inspect dressed bodies at actual game sizes.
5. Use measured sprint splits to constrain cumulative travel time at their corresponding distances, preserving start/timing protocol. Express acceleration and sustained speed separately. Jump displacement and direction-change performance need their own observations or declared artistic estimates. Do not infer endurance, reactive agility, exact force production or muscle size from a 40-yard time or bench-repetition count. Animation must preserve bone lengths, credible joint ranges and foot contacts.
6. Persist one recognizable person through haircuts, dye, beard changes, aging, dated mass/composition updates, trades and reloads. Capacity-only changes alter movement; documented physical-state changes alter the relevant body regions. Skin/eyes/natural hair and skeleton remain stable unless their own dated change is supported.
