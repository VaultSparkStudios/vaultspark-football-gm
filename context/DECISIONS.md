## 2026-07-02 — Session 29: additive situational play-calling over drive-engine rewrite

**Decision:** Implement fourth-down decision-making and situational play selection as a real, tracked-within-the-drive down/distance/field-position state machine layered on top of the existing drive-summary engine, rather than rearchitecting `simulateDrive` into a full down-by-down play-by-play engine.

**Rationale:** The audit's literal ask ("no down-and-distance awareness, no 4th-down go-for-it model") is real, but the drive engine's yardage/completion/sack/interception formulas are tuned against a heavily-guarded regression suite (calibration, monte-carlo, stats, ratings, career-realism, determinism). A full rearchitecture in one session risks destabilizing calibration that took many prior sessions to tune, with no safety net beyond re-tuning tolerances after the fact. The additive design tracks real down/distance/field position, replaces the flat coin-flip play selector with a situational one, and adds a genuine go/kick/punt fourth-down brain — reusing the exact existing FG-make and punt-yardage formulas for the forced-4th-down path, and leaving the natural-end-of-drive resolution path (for drives that exhaust their rolled play budget before a real 4th down) completely untouched byte-for-byte.

**Verification:** Full calibration/monte-carlo/stats/ratings/career-realism/determinism regression suite passed unchanged before and after; a new end-to-end test proves forced fourth-down kicks/punts fire in real simulated games.

**Pattern established:** When an audit item's literal scope would require rearchitecting a heavily-calibrated system, prefer an additive layer that changes *decisions* (what play is called, whether to go for it) without touching the *formulas* that produce the outcome distributions those decisions feed into. Verify with the full regression suite, not just the new feature's own tests.

Deleting the copy would have removed a real design beat to make a false statement true; enforcing it makes the statement true instead. The UI now derives its window from the declared value, so the surfaces cannot drift apart again.

**Decision 2 — resolve the lifecycle registry drift upstream, never locally.** The authoritative registry carries `vaultStatus: sparked` while the local contract expects FORGE. The obvious "reconciliation" — flipping the local contract — would have turned `launch-blocker`, `public-status` and `launch-evidence` into `ok: true / "not required"`, because all three are written as `expected !== "FORGE" || …`. Three blocking gates would have gone silently vacuous with doctor still reporting `blockingFailing: 0`. The registry is also self-contradictory (it says `sparked` and `public-unlaunched` at once) and the project holds three unmet launch requirements, so FORGE is the correct value. Shipped as Ark `registry-delta` cargo (id `01K23TUORM2357E166D84319EC`); no sibling tree was edited. **Pattern:** when a drift check offers a one-line "fix" that makes several other checks stop applying, the one-line fix is the defect.

**Decision 3 — defer three measured engine defects rather than half-fix them.** Signing-bonus proration, the unbounded free-agent pool, and the out-of-range dispersion arm are all real and all measured (numbers on the task board). Each needs a save-shape migration or a 10-season re-measurement budget this session could not honestly fund alongside the persistence work it was already landing. **Rationale:** the dispersion item in particular would turn the canonical suite red the moment its assertion is wired, and shipping a red gate in the same session as a deploy would either block the deploy or invite forcing it green. Recording the measurement is worth more than a partial fix that moves the number without closing the class. Honest deferral, not a silent skip.

**Pattern established — every new gate gets a negative control against the real pre-fix defect.** Four gates were added or repaired this session, and each was proven to go red on the actual defect it exists to catch before its green was believed. This caught one false green in the act: the first draft of the cap-rollover test passed with the bug restored, because the fixture's payroll left enough space that both the correct and the incorrect reading hit the same ceiling. It was rewritten until it failed, then re-verified. A gate whose red has never been observed is a gate with an unknown denominator.



## 2026-09-09 — S102 — Fix the arm, not the target; publish the divergence you decline to act on

**Decision 1 — correct the dispersion arm's population; leave the parity target's alone.** Both looked like the same defect and only one was. `LEAGUE_DISTRIBUTION_TARGET` declares "active-roster overall dispersion and elite density" and its dispersion arm was reading the blended active-roster-plus-practice-squad population, while its own elite arm had been moved to `activeRosterOnly` in S92 — an implementation disagreeing with its declaration, and a real bug. `LEAGUE_PROGRESSION_PARITY_TARGET` declares "rostered-player mean overall annual drift" and measures `rostered`, which is what it says. **Rationale:** during implementation the parity arm was moved to the active roster too, and then reverted, because the two cases are not alike. Changing the distribution arm makes an implementation match its declared metric; changing the parity arm would change what the gate *is* — and the fact that doing so turns it from `on-target` (+0.055/season) to `watch` (+0.282/season) is precisely why it must not happen as a side effect of a bug fix. From outside, "I re-pointed the gate at a different population and it went red" and "I moved a threshold until the number fit" are the same act performed in opposite directions. **A declared target is changed deliberately, as its own item, or not at all.**

**Decision 2 — publish the reading we declined to gate.** The active-roster mean drift is emitted on every parity receipt as `activeRosterMeanOverallDrift`, explicitly `gated: false` and carrying `wouldClassifyAs`, and the pre-S102 blended dispersion reading is emitted as `distribution.blendedRostered` the same way. **Rationale:** this class of defect has now recurred three times in this repo, and each time the cost was not that the wrong number was computed but that the *contrast* went unrecorded, so the next session had to rediscover it. Declining to act on a measurement is defensible; letting it evaporate is not. The convention is the one `splitActivePopulation` already set for `unrostered`: the population that is not gated stays visible.

**Decision 3 — a hypothesis inherited from the previous session is evidence, not instruction.** S101 handed over a specific root cause for the dispersion defect: the generator's SUPERSTAR band, with a recommendation to retune it. Measurement disproved it — the sd is not diffusing on the declared population at all (4.318 → 4.690 over ten seasons); the arm was reading a different population. Had the recommendation been implemented as written, a calibration constant would have been retuned to fix a defect that did not exist, and the real one would have survived under a green gate. **Pattern:** a handoff's *measurements* are inherited; its *diagnoses* are re-derived. This is the second time in two sessions that running the engine contradicted a plausible reading of it.

**Decision 4 — pay for the feature in bytes, not in budget.** The bye-week work pushed the initial shell to 651,225 against a 650,000 target. Trimming this session's own comments recovered 398 bytes and would not have reached; the budget was not raised. The trade-deadline frenzy board became a declared lazy root instead — its call site was already guarded to the declared deadline window, so only its download was ungated — landing at 642,587 with 7,413 bytes of headroom against the 848 inherited. **Rationale:** the standing rule is that a red budget is fixed by moving markup behind a dynamic import, never by raising `maxBytes`, and a module used in a handful of weeks a season but downloaded on every boot is the exact shape that rule is for. Shaving documentation until the number squeaks under would have paid the same cost in a worse currency.

**Decision 5 — a negative control can be green and prove nothing.** This session's `derivedRng` control measured a single draw per seed key and reported no meaningful clustering, so it failed to reproduce a defect that was demonstrably present. The defect compounds across the *two* draws a name consumes; rewritten around the pair statistic it reports 13/32 against the fixed source's 31/32. **Pattern:** a negative control must reproduce the defect *in the shape it was observed*, not in the simplest shape that resembles it. S101 established that every gate needs a negative control; S102 adds that the control needs its own statistic checked against the original measurement.

## 2026-09-10 — S103 — Decide the denominator on evidence, then refuse to ship the answer at a threshold's expense

**Decision 1 — the denominator question has a factual answer, and `rostered` fails it.** S102 declined to re-point this target and asked first: is the league the gate polices the one the GM competes in, or the one the roster rules define? Neither — `rostered` fails a prior test. **A drift statistic requires a population that exists at both ends of its window.** A generated league starts with an empty practice squad and fills 16 slots per club over the following decade, so the denominator grows by roughly a quarter of itself and everything it gains sits about ten points below the roster it is averaged into. Shift-share on the canonical seed: within-group +0.269/season (`watch`), between-group −0.197/season, blended +0.072 and reported `on-target`. **Rationale:** two effects of opposite sign cancelling inside a gate built to catch that is the third instance of this shape here — S91 (the free-agent pool in this same denominator), S102 (the dispersion arm), now this. The gated number was not slightly wrong; it was answering a different question.

**Decision 2 — and the re-point still does not ship, because it turns the gate red.** Pointing the target at `activeRosterOnly` reads **0.303/season** on the canonical seed: `out-of-range` against `watchMaxAbs` 0.3, not `watch`. A red gate has exactly two honest exits — fix the defect it found, or weaken the gate — and weakening is forbidden here by the project's own standing rule. **Rationale:** having the right argument for a re-point is necessary and not sufficient; you also have to be able to pay for it. Shipping it would have forced a threshold change in the same commit, which from outside is indistinguishable from moving a threshold until the number fits, and would have converted a correct diagnosis into the exact failure S102 wrote its deferral to avoid. `onTargetMaxAbs` and `watchMaxAbs` are untouched. **The finding ships instead of the re-point:** the realism regression now asserts that `compositionShift` reports `verdict-changed-by-composition` on the canonical path, so the gate cannot report `on-target` without also reporting that its denominator moved underneath it. That is the durable half, and it did not cost a threshold.

**Decision 3 — the guard's criterion is a changed verdict, not a magnitude.** `buildCompositionShift`'s first draft flagged composition when `|between| > |within|`. On the exact measurement that motivated the whole item it reported "development-dominated", because 0.197 is not larger than 0.269 — it would have shipped green on the defect it was written for. It now classifies the blended drift and the within-group drift against the target's own bands and reports disagreement. **Pattern:** S101 established that every gate needs a negative control and S102 added that the control must reproduce the defect in the shape it was observed. S103 adds the corollary for the *gate*: run the finished gate against the real measurement, not only against a fixture built to trip it. No new free parameter was introduced — the classification bands already existed and now do the work.

**Decision 4 — the recommended ratchet fix was built, measured against a matched control, and reverted.** S102 handed over age-indexed potential decline for elite density, reasoning that a ceiling which never falls makes reversion a permanent floor under every veteran. Implemented exactly as described, then run against an otherwise identical control for ten seasons on seed 2026. Mean potential fell 0.87 — the cost lands — while elite density did not move (3.80% → 3.92%) and the parity reading got worse (+0.265 → +0.298/season). The age mix explains it: the active roster's mean age falls 27.38 → 26.0 over the window and the elite cohort averages 26.6, so the rule reaches about a third of its target and replaces what it reaches with younger, higher-potential intake. **Rationale:** keeping it on "but it is more realistic" would be an unmeasured judgement call standing in for the measurement I actually have. Reverted whole; no dead module retained. **Pattern:** third consecutive session in which running the engine contradicted a plausible inherited diagnosis. A handoff's measurements are inherited; its diagnoses, and now its *recommended fixes*, are re-derived.

**Decision 5 — check the third site rather than fix it for symmetry.** Two subsystems shared one league identity and were genuinely defective. `gmDecisionAuthority.normalizedFranchiseId` carries the same shape of fallback and is not: it keys an occurrence ledger held inside a single save's own state, so two franchises cannot collide through it. **Rationale:** a sweep for twins is how this project finds the rest of a defect class, but the sweep has to end in a verdict per site. Changing a correct call site because it *looks* like a defective one buys a diff and no behaviour, and moves the identity a save's dedupe ledger is keyed on for nothing.

**Decision 6 — verify the migration cost a handoff attaches to an item.** The league-identity item arrived labelled "wants a migration decision rather than an edit", on the reading that changing the staff seed key regenerates staff for every existing save. Reading the builder showed it preserves any stored name that is not the old role-label fallback and any finite stored attribute, and a real snapshot with its identity stripped confirmed it. **Pattern:** an inherited *cost estimate* is a diagnosis too. Deferring an item on an unverified cost is how a cheap fix stays deferred for sessions.

## 2026-09-10 — S104 — Generate the league at the structure its own rules declare

**Decision 1 — the question S102 asked and S103 answered was the wrong question.** It was framed as a choice between two gated populations. S103 showed `rostered` failed a prior test: a drift statistic requires a population that exists at both ends of its window. What no session tested is that **`activeRosterOnly` fails the same test, mirrored** — if a club holds 49 players at the start of the window and 69 at the end, the active roster is an *unfiltered 49* at one end and the *top 53 of 69* at the other, silently acquiring a selection filter it did not have. That is why it read 0.303. **Rationale:** neither candidate was a valid drift statistic, so choosing between them could not have produced one. The defect was one level below the question: `ROSTER_TEMPLATE` summed to 49 against a declared `activeLimit` of 53 and generation built no practice squad at all against a declared `practiceLimit` of 16, so every league in this project's history began 20 players a club below its own steady state. **Pattern:** when two candidate answers to a question both fail, stop choosing and check the premise the question shares.

**Decision 2 — the parity re-point is deliberately still not taken, and that is now a decision rather than an inheritance.** Both populations are valid drift statistics at last. `rostered` reads +0.038 to +0.063 across seeds against an `onTargetMaxAbs` of 0.15; `activeRosterOnly` reads +0.102 to +0.115. **Rationale:** S103 deferred the re-point because it could not pay for it. S104 can, and declines: moving a declared target for no measured reason trades a comfortable margin for a tight one and buys nothing. The contrast stays published, ungated, in `activeRosterMeanOverallDrift` so a future drift back toward 0.303 is visible. `onTargetMaxAbs` and `watchMaxAbs` remain untouched across all three sessions.

**Decision 3 — the honest cost is published, not absorbed.** The distribution gate's dispersion arm moved `on-target` → `watch` (+0.095/season on the canonical path, +0.105 and +0.111 on two probed seeds, against `stdDevDriftOnTargetMaxAbs` 0.08). **Rationale:** it is not a regression this session caused — it is the same artefact seen from the other side. The old `on-target` was produced by the active roster's selection filter *strengthening* across the window (an unfiltered 49 becoming the top 53 of 69), which compresses measured dispersion exactly as the underlying spread widens. With the filter constant, the arm reports the S91 random walk it was built to find. Closing it by widening `stdDevDrift*` would be weakening a gate to hide a reading the fix made honest. **Pattern:** a fix that removes an artefact will make some gates look worse. That is the gate starting to work, and it is a WIN to record, not a red to close.

**Decision 4 — a minimum does not stop an accumulation; a maximum does.** The first design for the fieldable roster declared only per-position floors. Nothing in a floor stops a sixth quarterback out-rating a fourth cornerback and taking the slot on merit, which is exactly how the quarterback room went 64 → 195 over ten seasons. `FIELDABLE_DEPTH` declares a `min` **and** a `max`, and legality outranks both so a structural preference can never shrink a roster below the 53 a club must dress. **Pattern:** if you are declaring a floor to fix an accumulation, name the term that stops the accumulation before you ship.

**Decision 5 — a fieldability guard protects a count, not people.** The first version computed each club's best `min` players at every position and made those individuals unreleasable. Driven on the S91 camp-cuts fixture at seed 20260817, a club **$129.0M over the cap** carried two of its three monstrous contracts on "protected" players, bottomed out at the 53-man floor still **$27.8M over**, and reported itself trapped. It was not trapped; it had eleven other linemen. **Rationale:** what a club may not do is drop *below* the minimum at a position. Who fills that minimum is a personnel decision the club is entitled to get wrong. **Pattern:** a constraint on a roster's *shape* implemented as a constraint on named individuals is a different rule with a different failure mode, and the fixture that catches the difference is a club that must reach legality, not one that comfortably can.

**Decision 6 — a fix for a moving denominator installed a new moving denominator, twice, and both were caught by measuring rather than reasoning.** `teamNeeds` measured the club's *active* roster against `ROSTER_TEMPLATE`; once the merit pass began allocating discretionary slots, whichever room lost the pass reported a permanent shortfall and sent the free-agency backstop shopping every offseason. Correcting it to `FIELDABLE_DEPTH[pos].min` was worse: the minimums sum to 41, clubs restocked to 41, the league drained **2,208 → 1,905** over ten seasons and the practice squad fell from 16 a club to 6.5 — **the S103 defect with its sign flipped, installed by the fix for it.** The floor is `FULL_ROSTER_TEMPLATE`, the roster a club actually carries. **Pattern:** after removing an accumulator, re-measure the same window for a new one before believing the fix. Two of the three drafts here were reasoned and wrong.

**Decision 7 — the salary curve was not shaved to make the roster fit.** Filling the declared roster put all 32 clubs over a $255M cap before a snap was played (median space −$50.1M) against a baseline of zero over-cap clubs. **Rationale:** shaving `marketSalaryForOverall` until the number fits is moving a threshold, and that curve is bound to `CONTRACT_RULES.maxSalary` by test. A real roster does not pay 53 market salaries — it pays its starters and key backups and fills the rest at the minimum, and `FIELDABLE_DEPTH[pos].min` is already this project's declaration of who those players are. The residual is closed with more of the same rule (another minimum deal, dearest contract outside the fieldable minimum first), bounded by the number of demotable players, so it either reaches legality or leaves the club visibly over for the compliance pass to see.

**Decision 8 — a gate that cannot fail is indistinguishable from a gate that passes.** `scripts/lib/brief-preflight.mjs` has spawned `scripts/check-audit-premises.mjs` inside `try { … } catch { /* non-fatal */ }` since the premise-decay step was propagated here, and **the file was not in the repository**. No cache was ever written, the SIGNALS row never appeared, and every typed `premises` block in `docs/AUDIT_*.json` went unchecked for the project's whole history — with nothing to say so. **Rationale:** the step is correct to be advisory; a stale audit must not block a brief render. But advisory has to mean "reports a problem without blocking", never "reports nothing". The checker reuses `isAuditComplete` rather than declaring a fourth copy of the completion vocabulary, and treats an unobservable premise as a third outcome so a renamed target can never read as "the defect is fixed". **Pattern:** a silent `catch` around a spawn makes a missing dependency and a clean run the same observation. Audit the catch, not only the code inside it.

**Decision 9 — the general form of a defect is worth more than the two instances, when it is checkable by reading.** Two propagated scripts were referenced and absent, and each hid behind a different mechanism: `check-audit-premises.mjs` behind a silent `catch` around a spawn, `check-last-session-summary.mjs` behind a static import in `scripts/lib/brief-preflight.mjs` — a module that consequently could not load at all, and that nothing in this repo imports, so its `ERR_MODULE_NOT_FOUND` was never raised by anything. **Rationale:** writing the two missing files closes two instances; asserting that every static relative import under `scripts/` resolves closes the class, and does it without executing a single runtime path. Comments are stripped before matching, because usage examples in docstrings name modules relative to the *caller* rather than to the file they are written in — with that, the scan returned exactly one hit and no false positives. Dynamic and computed specifiers are deliberately out of scope rather than approximated, because a gate that guesses is a gate whose reds have to be argued about.

**Decision 10 — the `lastSessionSummary` writer heals only from prose that names the right session, and `currentSession` was reconciled rather than worked around.** `render-startup-brief` reads the summary against `currentSession - 1`, so `currentSession` is the session *about to run*. S103 left it at 103 while its own `currentFocus` described S103, which is why the arithmetic never lined up and the summary sat at S100 for four sessions. **Rationale:** the tempting fix is to make the writer copy `currentFocus` unconditionally — the detector goes green immediately. That replaces a *visibly* stale summary with an *invisibly* wrong one, which is strictly worse for the only person the block exists to serve. It heals when `currentFocus` names the expected session and reports honestly when it does not, and the counter it depends on is set to a true value instead of one that happens to satisfy the check. **Pattern:** when a detector and a writer disagree about what a field means, fix the meaning; do not teach the writer to satisfy the detector.

**Decision 11 — CORRECTED IN-SESSION. There is no closeout `currentSession` bump, and the invariant I first recorded here did not exist.** The original entry claimed that `render-closeout-board.mjs` (which reads `status.currentSession` as the session it is closing out) and `render-startup-brief.mjs` (which computes `expectedSession = currentSession - 1`) are reconciled only by bumping the counter as closeout's final write. I bumped it to 105, re-rendered the brief to check the fix, and **the brief rendered Session 106** — because `currentSession` is not a hand-set field at all. `resolveSessionAuthority` derives the next session from the **monotonic maximum of every committed authority** and self-heals `status.currentSession` at session start; my bump was read as another committed authority and pushed the maximum forward. Reset to 104, the brief renders `Session 105` with `session-authority: committed=S104 · SIL=S104 · status=S104; divergent=false` and no stale block. **Rationale for recording the error rather than the corrected conclusion alone:** the wrong version was written into DECISIONS, TRUTH_AUDIT, the handoff, the closeout brief and a source docstring before a two-minute empirical check disproved it, and the check was only run because the fix was being verified end to end rather than declared. `currentSession` at closeout equals the session that just ran; the brief advances it. **Pattern:** a field with a derivation function is not a field you set. Before writing down an invariant about who owns a value, find the code that computes it — and when the invariant is cheap to test, test it instead of reasoning about it.

**Decision 11b — so the actual cause of the four-session staleness is simpler than the ordering story.** `lastSessionSummary` had a detector and no writer, and no closeout hand-wrote it either. That is the whole defect. It is closed by writing the summary at closeout, which is this project's declared authority for that field, and it stays closed as long as each closeout writes it.

**Decision 12 — the studio shard was right and the fix was wrong: delete the file, do not allowlist it.** Having found that `scripts/lib/brief-preflight.mjs` statically imports an absent `scripts/check-last-session-summary.mjs`, I wrote that module. `test/project-authority-contract.test.js` failed it immediately: the path is named in `context/PROJECT_AUTHORITY_CONTRACT.json` under `forbiddenFiles`, whose stated purpose is to *"fail closed when generic Studio propagation erases project-specific authority contracts"*, and this project declares exactly three permitted `statusMutators` — the new module would have been a fourth mutator of `PROJECT_STATUS.json`. **Rationale:** the file was not missing by oversight, it is *excluded by contract*, and the preflight is generic machinery that depends on something this project deliberately does not have. Adding an allowlist entry would have converted a working gate into a formality to make my own change pass, which is the precise move this project forbids everywhere else. It was deleted; the defect is closed by this project's own authority — `lastSessionSummary` written at closeout, by hand, plus the `currentSession` reconciliation — and the check that had been red for four sessions is green on true values. **Pattern:** when a gate you did not write fails your change, the first hypothesis is that the gate is right. Read what it is protecting before touching it.

**Decision 13 — the general gate encodes the true invariant, which is not the convenient one.** The naive rule — every static relative import under `scripts/` must resolve — would have *required* creating the forbidden file to go green. The rule that is actually true, and that ships: every static relative import resolves **unless** its target is contractually forbidden, in which case the importing module must have **no importer**, so the forbidden dependency can never be exercised. **Rationale:** the second clause is the load-bearing one. It states the real property (`brief-preflight` is inert here by design) and it fails loudly the moment someone wires it up, which is exactly when the contract would otherwise be defeated at runtime. A gate that can only be satisfied by violating another gate is not a gate; it is a trap.

**Decision 14 — the release-note debt was two sessions old and the tolerance is why nobody saw it.** `check-public-truth` counts sessions rather than days and tolerates exactly one, on the reasoning that the note lands at closeout so one behind is in flight. S103 shipped without a player-facing note and landed *exactly* on that tolerance, so the gate stayed green on a page that had already stopped tracking the product; it only fired when S104 made it two. **Rationale:** the note written is a real one — clubs dressing a legal depth chart instead of six quarterbacks and four kickers, new leagues starting at a full roster, the draft supplying the positions clubs need, the cap binding from the first snap — not a dated heading added to move a number. **Pattern:** a tolerance of one means a single skipped session is invisible by construction. That is the right trade for the gate, and it means the debt is always discovered one session late; write the note at the closeout that earns it.

## 2026-09-11 — S105 — A surface the brief reads must have a writer, and a test that fails when the writer is skipped

**Decision 1 — the handoff heading this project writes is committed session authority, and only its left-hand session counts.** `resolveSessionAuthority` takes the monotonic maximum of SIL, status and handoff and reports divergence when they disagree, but `parseHandoffCloseoutAuthority` knew only `# Session N Closeout` and `brief-semantic-fingerprint` carried its own literal for `Where We Left Off (Session N)`. This project has headed every handoff `# Latest Handoff — Session N → Session N+1` since at least S96 — checked against twelve committed handoffs, none matched — so the brief rendered `handoff=S?` for the project's whole recorded history and the divergence check ran on two sources. **Rationale:** the fix teaches the shared parser this project's shape rather than rewriting twelve handoffs into the studio shape, and routes the fingerprint through the same parser so the heading is declared once. `N` is the closed session; `M` is the next session's intent and never counts, or a handoff would advance the committed session by one on its own. A test binds the *live* handoff to the newest SIL entry, which makes the heading shape load-bearing: keep it. Shipped upstream as Ark cargo so propagation does not revert it.

**Decision 2 — the SIL rolling-status block is a projection of the entries, never an authority, and the averages it used to print were not reproducible.** The block was hand-maintained, last touched at S100, and never carried `Intent rate:`, so the brief showed `Intent: ?%` beside five consecutive `Intent outcome: Achieved` entries. `scripts/render-sil-rolling-status.mjs` now derives it from the live file and its archive. **The derived numbers differ from the hand-written ones and the derived ones are what ship:** Avg3 **959.7** against the hand-written **974.7**, and the hand-written block's "87 unique scored sessions" is not reproducible from `/1000` scores — the entries yield **58**. The difference is published in the block itself (which names the count it read) rather than reconciled by adopting a normalisation rule for older scales that nobody wrote down. The intent rate's denominator is entries that record an outcome; an entry that recorded none is unknown, not a miss. `Velocity` and `Debt` are not derivable from the entries and are carried, labelled as carried. **Pattern:** a header that summarises a ledger and is maintained by hand will drift from the ledger; derive it, and bind the committed copy to a fresh derivation.

**Decision 3 — open premise decay across the whole audit history now fails the studio shard.** `check-audit-premises.mjs` is advisory and reads the newest sidecar only, and its one caller cannot be imported here by contract, so S104's "zero open decay across all sidecars" was a one-off manual reading. The corpus assertion walks every `docs/AUDIT_*.json` and has a fixture negative control. **On its first run it went red on a live instance — this session's own sidecar**, whose first three items had shipped while still marked `planned`, which is exactly the decay it exists to see. Resolved by recording the items as implemented with their evidence, not by exempting the current sidecar. **Pattern:** a gate that fires on its author's own work in the first minute is the strongest negative control available; keep the evidence of it.

**Decision 4 — `render-startup-brief` will not import `brief-preflight`, and the question is closed rather than carried a fourth time.** `brief-preflight.mjs` statically imports `scripts/check-last-session-summary.mjs`, which `context/PROJECT_AUTHORITY_CONTRACT.json` forbids, and S104's import gate fails the suite the moment anything imports it. The answer was determined by the contract; leaving it open as a "decide later" item was decision debt. The module stays in the tree, inert by design, because zero-importer files here have repeatedly turned out to be unwired assets and this one is generic machinery other projects use.

**Decision 5 — the PROJECT PROFILE row is studio-level debt and ships as cargo, not as a local edit.** The row reads `.cache/project-profile.json`, a 30-minute TTL cache written only by studio-ops' `scripts/lib/project-profile.mjs`, which this repo does not carry; the brief is rendered at closeout, so the row is structurally `refresh required`. Both the renderer and the lens are propagated, so a local fix would be overwritten and a sibling-tree edit is forbidden.
