## Premise verification and rejected phantom work

- Rejected/deferred “Add another generic franchise subsystem or dashboard”: Rejected as additive sprawl. The live product already has deep roster, cap, draft, weekly, season, and dynasty authorities; this pass strengthens decision quality and evidence boundaries.
- Rejected/deferred “Claim improvement from local playtest ratings”: Rejected because the receipts are local, self-selected, and below any consented cohort standard. Implementation may repair consent and truth, not claim impact.
- Rejected/deferred “Add streaks, random rewards, or tactic-variety bonuses”: Rejected as coercive or contradictory to the deliberate-identity canon recorded in DECISIONS.
- Rejected/deferred “Add a hosted large-language-model coach”: Rejected because it introduces variable free-tier cost and data custody without stronger evidence than a deterministic source-derived critique can provide.
- Rejected/deferred “Treat local green evidence as launch approval”: Rejected. Hosted provenance, edge health, received email, approval, and lifecycle reconciliation remain independent evidence gates.
- Rejected/deferred “Rewrite the app shell because files are large”: Rejected as a phantom premise. The demonstrated cost is eager loader fan-out, so the root fix belongs at the hydration dispatcher.
- Rejected/deferred “Alarm on Max Plan token cost”: Rejected by the founder's flat-rate notional-cost rule. The measurable optimization target is browser request fan-out, not model spend.
- Rejected/deferred “Refresh release reports or edit the sibling registry to make gates green”: Rejected by root-fix, observability-honesty, and Ark ownership rules.

## Three recommended design moves

1. Make local feedback attachment explicit and source-true before asking players to publish free text.
2. Give the weekly command one joined pre-commit rehearsal so the player can challenge or revise the plan before simulation.
3. Hydrate secondary panels by visited-tab demand instead of issuing twenty-four startup loads.
