# Audit — Franchise Architect: Football — Session 67

Public-safe live-code audit. The JSON sidecar is the sole source of truth.

## Profile and review lens

- Product: public-unlaunched public browser football game/app
- Rubric: product rubric with game-medium overlay: retention, emotional payoff, fast playable proof, mobile browser polish, vivid General Manager decision pressure, zero-backend loops; staging: GitHub Pages artifact with exact same-origin hosted verification before direct-to-main promotion
- Profile source: live execution-path reading of the offseason pipeline, the draft authority, the pick-asset ledger, the trade service and the free-agency market, with every premise re-proven by running the real engine in-process rather than by grep
- Game-loop review: tightness 9 · progression 5.5 · session engagement 8.4 · retention 6.5 · soul fidelity 7.4 · overall 7.4
- Evidence caveat: The in-season loop is genuinely strong and twenty sessions of work show. The offseason — the half of a franchise game where a GM actually builds a team — is where this audit found the floor missing. Driving the real pipeline in-process: the free-agent pool is exactly 0 players at every one of the seven offseason stages, while 295 contracts (239 of them 74+ overall) expire inside a single synchronous call at the `udfa` stage and 219 of those players are handed to CPU clubs in the same tick, before the market the player bids in has ever been consulted. Progression and retention are marked down hard because roster building — the reason to start season two — currently happens to the player rather than by them. Soul fidelity drops from S63's 8.6 because the franchise's most consequential decisions are the ones it does not let you make.

## Premise verification and rejected phantom work

- Rejected/deferred “`runCpuDraft` auto-picks for the controlled team when `allowTop10PickTrading` is false — inverted challenge logic”: Rejected as a phantom. Read in isolation the branch at GameSession.js:4926 looks inverted, but GameSession.js:4886 blocks the *user* from selecting inside the top 10 under the same flag with an explicit message ('Trade down or let the CPU resolve the pick'). The two branches agree; the behaviour is deliberate. Separately noted and not raised as work: no entry in CHALLENGE_MODES actually sets `allowTop10PickTrading: false`, so the restriction is currently unreachable configuration rather than a defect.
- Rejected/deferred “Playoff seeding and division tiebreakers are naive”: Rejected. `src/engine/seasonSimulator.js:28-89` implements head-to-head, division and conference record tiebreakers with a proper tie-group walk. Premise false against live code.
- Rejected/deferred “Mark launch or lifecycle green from this repository”: Rejected. Hosted health, applied edge headers, received mailbox evidence, founder approval and sibling lifecycle truth are independent authorities. The doctor warning 'registry SPARKED · local contract FORGE' is sibling-owned drift, correctly non-blocking, and is not to be resolved by editing a sibling tree.
- Rejected/deferred “General Manager firing / job-security termination arc”: Deferred again, honestly. Carried from S62 and S63 as founder creative direction, unchanged. Recording the deferral rather than silently skipping it.
- Rejected/deferred “Memoize `getDashboardState()`”: Deferred. Measured at 24.7 ms per build on a live session — real but not a player-visible cost at current polling rates, and a cache introduces a staleness class of bug directly against the S49 authority-keyed hydration fences. Not worth the risk this session; recorded with its measurement so a future session inherits the number rather than the guess.

## Three recommended design moves

1. Split the monolithic `runOffseason()` blob out of the `udfa` stage and let each named pipeline stage do the thing it is named for — the fix is an ordering fix, not new simulation.
2. Open a real free-agency window between expiry and the draft, resolved by the S62 competing-offer market that has been shipped and unreachable for five sessions.
3. Derive the draft order from `league.draftPicks[].ownerTeamId` plus compensatory picks, and consume each pick as it is used, so a traded first round pick is actually gone.
