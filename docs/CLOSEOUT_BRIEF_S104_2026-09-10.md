# Closeout Brief — franchise-architect — S104

> Two sessions were spent choosing between two candidate populations for one gate. Both were invalid, for the same reason: a league was never generated at the roster its own rules declare.

## Shipped

- **A league is generated at the structure its own rules declare** (10/10 project, 8/10 ecosystem): ROSTER_TEMPLATE summed to 49 against a declared activeLimit of 53, and generation built no practice squad at all against a declared practiceLimit of 16. Every league in this project's history began 20 players a club below its own steady state and closed the gap over the following decade. That is why neither candidate gated population was a valid drift statistic: rostered gained about 39 per cent of itself mid-window (the S103 finding), and activeRosterOnly silently acquired a selection filter as an unfiltered 49 became the top 53 of 69 — the same defect mirrored, and the reason the re-point read 0.303. PRACTICE_SQUAD_TEMPLATE and a raised ROSTER_TEMPLATE now sum to exactly the declared limits, and FULL_ROSTER_TEMPLATE holds clubs there as a floor in teamNeeds rather than only as a ceiling in the compliance pass. Measured on the canonical seed 20260306 over ten seasons: rostered population 1568 to 2178 becomes 2208 to 2183; practice weight 0 to 22.7 per cent becomes 23.2 to 22.7; between-group drift -0.197 becomes +0.003; within-group +0.269 watch becomes +0.040 on-target; activeRosterOnly +0.303 out-of-range becomes +0.107 on-target.
- **The active 53 is a fieldable depth chart, not a leaderboard** (9/10 project, 7/10 ecosystem): normalizeRosterSlots labelled the top 53 of a club's roster by overall with no positional structure, and overall is not comparable across positions — quarterbacks and specialists rate several points above a league mean by construction. Measured over ten seasons on the canonical seed, quarterback rooms went 64 to 195 players and specialists 64 to 155 while the offensive line fell 288 to 258 and the front seven 480 to 407, with 11.3 per cent of quarterbacks at 90+ overall. Clubs were dressing six quarterbacks and four kickers. FIELDABLE_DEPTH declares a minimum AND a maximum per room and assignFieldableActiveRoster allocates in three passes: minimums first, then merit bounded by each room's ceiling, then legality. A minimum alone would not have fixed it — nothing in a minimum stops a sixth quarterback out-rating a fourth cornerback and taking the slot, which is exactly how the room grew. The selection is exported pure, so the property is asserted on a roster directly rather than by simulating a decade, and its negative control reproduces the pre-fix ranking explicitly and shows the two rules disagree.
- **Neither release path can cut a club below a roster it could field** (8/10 project, 7/10 ecosystem): The roster-limit cut released the tail by overall and the cap cut released by value density — overall per dollar of cap hit — and neither scalar is position-neutral. A quarterback or kicker on a rookie deal has enormous overall-per-dollar; a starting left tackle has very little. So the club that had to get legal did it by cutting linemen, every time. selectReleasable walks the existing release order and refuses only the move that would drop a room below its declared minimum, evaluated as the cuts accumulate. The release order itself is unchanged, so the front office's judgement is untouched. Proved with a negative control that reproduces the pre-S104 cap loop on a structuredClone and asserts it really does leave the club unable to field a line.
- **Draft intake is proportional to roster demand rather than uniform** (8/10 project, 6/10 ecosystem): createDraftClass sampled rng.pick over the nine drafted positions, so every position received 11.1 per cent of every draft class — 256 prospects a year, forever — while a club's demand for them ranges from 1.9 per cent (kicker) to 18.9 per cent (offensive line). Intake that does not match demand does not equilibrate; it accumulates on one side and starves the other, monotonically, and the measured ordering is exact: quarterbacks +230 per cent, specialists +169 per cent, offensive line +9 per cent, front seven +16 per cent over ten seasons. It also explains why the surplus concentrated in the highest-rated rooms. DRAFT_POSITION_WEIGHTS derives from the declared template and draws through the RNG's existing weightedPick, so exactly one value is drawn per prospect as before and a seeded league's RNG position is unchanged in count. Punters are drafted at last; excluding them while every club needs one is why the only route to a punter was a manufactured emergency-depth signing.
- **Every generated club can afford the roster it is generated with** (7/10 project, 6/10 ecosystem): Filling the declared roster on ordinary market contracts put all 32 clubs over a 255M cap before a snap was played — median space -50.1M, worst -84.7M — against a measured baseline of zero over-cap clubs and 43.4M median headroom. The compliance pass would have laundered it by cutting twenty players a club in the first offseason, so nothing would have gone red; the league would simply have started as fiction, which is the fiction S89 built the compliance authority to remove. The fix is not to shave the salary curve until the number fits: that is moving a threshold, and the curve is bound to CONTRACT_RULES.maxSalary by test. A real roster pays its starters and key backups and fills the rest at the league minimum, and FIELDABLE_DEPTH[pos].min is already this project's declaration of who those players are. The residual is closed with more of the same rule — another minimum deal, dearest contract outside the fieldable minimum first — bounded by the number of demotable players, so it either reaches legality or leaves the club visibly over for the compliance pass to see. Asserted across four seeds rather than on the canonical one alone.
- **The startup brief's premise-decay check was spawning a script that did not exist** (6/10 project, 9/10 ecosystem): scripts/lib/brief-preflight.mjs has spawned scripts/check-audit-premises.mjs inside a try/catch marked non-fatal since the premise-decay step was propagated to this repo, and the script was not in the repository at all. A missing dependency and a clean run were the same observation from outside: no .cache/audit-premise-decay.json was ever written, the brief's SIGNALS row never appeared, and every typed premises block in docs/AUDIT_*.json went unchecked for the project's whole history with nothing to say so. The checker reuses isAuditComplete rather than declaring a fourth copy of the completion vocabulary S100 built precisely because three readers had drifted apart on it; it treats an unobservable premise as a third outcome so a renamed target can never read as the defect being fixed; and it is advisory by default with --strict for a caller that wants a gate. Run across the entire audit history it reports zero unverified and zero open decay, and a fixture-driven negative control proves it goes red on an untouched item whose premise has flipped.

Deleted, not allowlisted. So the accurate finding is that brief-preflight is generic machinery depending on a file this project deliberately forbids, and is inert here by design. What closes the four-session staleness is this project's own authority: lastSessionSummary written at closeout by hand, plus reconciling currentSession, which S103 left one behind and which the brief's currentSession - 1 arithmetic depends on. The check is green on true values.
- **The general form, shipped as a gate that reads rather than executes - with the true invariant** (5/10 project, 9/10 ecosystem): The two absent modules hid behind two different mechanisms - a silent catch around a spawn, and a static import in a module with no importer - and both are provable without exercising a single runtime path. Across 150 modules.
