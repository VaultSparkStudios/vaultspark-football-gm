How it compares

There are many good football management games, and they are not all trying to do the same thing. This page says plainly what Franchise Architect: Football is, what it is not, and when another kind of game will suit you better. We describe kinds of game rather than naming products, because we would rather be fair than score points.

What this game is

A general manager game. You run the front office of one club in a fictional 32-club league: the roster, the contracts and salary cap, scouting and the draft, trades, the coaching staff and the owner's expectations. You do not call plays or control players on the field. Games are simulated, and you can watch the key plays afterwards.

Where it is strong

  • It is free, with no account. Nothing to buy, nothing to install and no sign-up. No ads. More in the FAQ.
  • Saves protect themselves. Your league saves in your browser with rolling automatic backups and rewind points, and you can export a save file at any time. How saves work.
  • An advisor that keeps score. Each week the Front Office Advisor argues one call and shows the figures behind it, then tracks how often you went against it and what happened next. About the Advisor.
  • Rivals that remember. Each of the 31 rival general managers values players in their own way and remembers the deals you have done together. About rival front offices.
  • An owner and a business. Facilities, ticket prices, a coaching market and an owner whose patience can run out. About the Boardroom.
  • Realism with its limits published. We say what the simulation is checked against and where that comparison stops working. The Simulation.

Where it is limited

  • No online multiplayer. Commissioner mode lets up to four general managers share one league, but only on the same device.
  • No real players or teams. The league, clubs and players are fictional. The game is not affiliated with any real league.
  • It runs only in a web browser. There is no console version and no app-store app. It does work in the browser on a phone or tablet.
  • You do not play the games. There is no play-calling and no on-field control.
  • It is in early access. The game changes often, and simulated results can differ from one version to the next. Open problems are listed under known issues.

Next to other kinds of football management game

These are general descriptions. Individual games in each group differ, so check the one you have in mind.

Spreadsheet-style browser sims

Usually free, fast, and built around tables of numbers. They tend to be very good at simulating many seasons quickly.

Shared ground: free, in the browser, with long careers.

This game puts an advisor, an owner and rivals with a memory at the centre, so there is more to read each week.

Choose that kind if you mainly want the tables and as little as possible between you and the next season.

Paid PC sims

Usually bought once or each year and installed on a computer. Many have been developed for a long time and have large communities.

Shared ground: depth in contracts, scouting and long-term team building.

This game is free and needs no install. It is also younger and still changing.

Choose that kind if you want an online league with friends or a long-settled game.

Mobile management hybrids

Usually built for short sessions on a phone. Many mix team management with arcade play, timers or in-app purchases.

Shared ground: you can play on a phone.

This game has no purchases, no timers and no ads. It asks for longer, slower decisions.

Choose that kind if you want a few minutes of quick play at a time.

Console franchise modes

Usually one mode inside a larger game where you also play the matches yourself, often with licensed leagues and players.

Shared ground: running a club across many seasons.

This game is only the front office; there is no other mode. Its league is fictional.

Choose that kind if you want real teams, or want to play the games on the field.
