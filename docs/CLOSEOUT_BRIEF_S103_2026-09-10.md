- **The measured root-cause candidate: active-roster position composition is drifting hard**: normalizeRosterSlots ranks a club's whole roster by overall with no positional structure, so over ten seasons the quarterback room goes 64 to 195 players and specialists 64 to 155, while the offensive line falls 288 to 258 and the front seven 480 to 407 — and the quarterback room carries 11.3 per cent of its players at 90+. High-rated, cheap positions crowd out linemen, inflating the active-roster mean for a compositional reason one level below the practice-squad one. Strong candidate for both the 0.303 parity reading and the standing elite-density watch, and the thing to fix before the re-point.

Any next candidate must price that, and must be run against a matched control before it is believed.

## Honesty Ledger

- **The re-point was implemented, measured against the canonical gate, and reverted**: Having the right argument for moving a declared population is necessary and not sufficient — you also have to be able to pay for it. On the canonical seed the active-roster reading is 0.303/season, out-of-range rather than watch, so shipping the re-point would have forced a threshold change in the same commit. From outside that is indistinguishable from moving a threshold until the number fits, and it would have converted a correct diagnosis into the exact failure S102 wrote its deferral to avoid. onTargetMaxAbs and watchMaxAbs are untouched; the finding shipped instead of the re-point.
- **An early reading of the browser red as self-caused was wrong, and the correction is recorded**: The first hypothesis was that the league-identity change caused the intermittent failure, on a negative control that passed 10/10 with the change disabled against 8/10 with it. That is not evidence at that sample size — 10/10 has roughly an 11 per cent probability at a 20 per cent failure rate — and chasing it cost real time. What settled it was the product's own weekly-plan receipt inside the captured failure artifact, which named the bye directly. Read the artifact the failure already wrote before building a control around a guess.
- **This session's own new gate returned the wrong verdict on the real numbers before it shipped**: The composition guard passed a fixture negative control and then classified the production measurement as development-dominated — the exact defect it was written to catch — because 0.197 is not larger than 0.269. Caught by running the finished gate against the measurement that motivated it, and rewritten around a changed verdict rather than an invented magnitude threshold.
- **The recommended ratchet fix was built, measured against a matched control, and reverted**: S102 handed over age-indexed potential decline as the candidate for elite density. Implemented exactly as described — onset 27, 0.35 points per year past prime, capped at 3.0, RNG-free, applied at the reversion seam — then run against an otherwise identical control for ten seasons on seed 2026. Active-roster mean potential fell 83.78 to 82.91, so the cost lands; elite density did not move (3.80 to 3.92 per cent) and the parity reading got worse, +0.265 to +0.298 per season. Measured age mix at season 8 says why: the active roster's mean age falls 27.38 to 26.0 and the elite cohort averages 26.6, so the rule reaches about a third of its target and replaces what it reaches with younger, higher-potential intake. Reverted whole, with no dead module retained.
- **The identity fix legitimately moved a seeded fixture, and the seed was re-justified rather than the assertion relaxed**: Giving each league its own identity changes derived staff and owner profiles, applyStaffToCoaching feeds those into team.coaching, and coaching drives how a league develops — so every freshly generated league from a given seed now plays out differently. That surfaced as a real suite failure: rival-trade-offers' S102 negative control pinned seed 620083 to surface its offer past the Week 11 deadline, and it now surfaces at week 2. The test's own note prescribes the response — re-justify the seed, do not delete the control — so seeds were re-scanned against the live engine and 620097 was adopted at week 12, with the cause written into the test. The condition is genuinely seed-sensitive: of twenty seeds scanned, three reproduced it and two produced no offer inside fourteen weeks at all. Existing saves are unaffected, because the profile builders preserve any value already stored.
- **An inherited migration cost was checked and found false; a twin sweep ended in a verdict per site**: The league-identity item was carried forward labelled as needing a migration decision because changing the seed key regenerates staff for every existing save; buildStaffProfile preserves any stored name that is not the old role-label fallback and any finite stored attribute, confirmed on a real snapshot with its identity stripped. And of three call sites carrying the same fallback shape, gmDecisionAuthority's is not in the defect class — it keys an occurrence ledger held inside a single save's own state — so it was left untouched deliberately rather than changed for symmetry.
- **Measurement scope declared rather than implied**: The parity decomposition and the roster-composition finding are single-seed ten-season readings on the canonical seed 20260306. The elite-density comparison and the ratchet control arm are single-seed ten-season readings on seed 2026. The league-identity result is a three-seed reading. SIL scores are engineering assessments, not measured player outcomes; no real-cohort evidence exists.
