How to play: one full year

You are the general manager. You do not call plays; you build the club that plays them. A season runs week by week, and the offseason after it is where most franchises are won or lost.

  1. Start a league

    One click on the start page starts a balanced league with a random club. You can pick an era first (Modern leans on the pass, Balanced sits in the middle, Legacy grinds on the ground), choose your team, or open the advanced settings for the start year, difficulty, rules and a franchise archetype such as a rebuild or a cap-strapped contender. Drive mode is quicker for long careers; Play mode resolves more individual plays and fuller box scores.

  2. Set your opening

    Your first stop is the Desk. A new franchise gets a First Season Contract: five objectives a first-year general manager is judged on. You can also sign the Opening Contract, which sets your franchise identity and what the owner expects. Check the roster and depth chart under Team before the first game.

  3. Play the season, one week at a time

    Each week the Desk shows one clear next call and the Front Office Advisor's argument for it. When you advance you set a short weekly plan, then read what happened: the result, the key plays, League News and how your plan held up. Advance four weeks or a whole season to move faster; the game stops at anything that needs you.

  4. Work the market before the deadline

    Rival general managers call with offers and price players by their own archetype and needs. Shop players, build packages with draft picks and sign free agents. There is one trade deadline, and every club obeys it.

  5. Make the playoffs, face the review

    The postseason keeps the same rhythm, one game and one plan at a time, ending in a neutral-site championship. Then awards are handed out, the owner gives a verdict and your legacy records the season.

  6. Rebuild in the offseason

    Retirements and the development report, the coaching carousel, re-signing your own players, free agency in bidding waves, the combine and pro days, then the draft. Prospects arrive in scouting fog, and their size and workouts now matter on the field: wingspan, hands, frame and the Relative Athletic Score all change games.

  7. Keep going

    There is no final season. Climb six legacy tiers, build rivalries, retire numbers and send players to the Hall of Fame. Your league saves in your browser with rolling backups.

Glossary

Around the game

Desk
The first screen of your franchise. It shows your club, the calls waiting for you this week and the Front Office Advisor's argument.
Front Office Advisor
Each week it argues for one call and shows the figures it is reading. It keeps a tally of how often you agreed and how often you went against it. The tally carries no penalty either way.
First Season Contract
Five objectives given to a new franchise: sign a free agent, set a depth chart yourself, work the trade desk, reach the owner's win target, and carry a legal cap into the offseason. Each is ticked off from what you actually do, and the owner gives a verdict at the season review.
Boardroom
The screen for the owner's money and the business: ticket prices and the staff budget, an operating statement of what the club earns and spends, facilities, the coaching staff and your club's brand.
Weekly plan
What you set when you advance a week: an optional general manager decision and a tactical focus for the game.
Standing orders
Your rules for two game-day moments. On fourth down choose Conservative, By the book or Aggressive. For the two-minute drill choose Protect the ball, Standard or Hurry-up. They stay until you change them.
Sim-Watch
A replay of the key plays of a game, shown as a broadcast with play-by-play. Open it by choosing one of your games in the box-score strip at the top of the screen. You can pause it, change the speed, step between key plays, skip to the final score or watch the Final Reel.
League Pulse
On the League screen. It ranks every club each week, with arrows for who climbed and who slid, and tracks the races for the season awards.
GM Legacy
Your record as a general manager across every franchise in this browser. It has six tiers: Young Gun, Contender Builder, Proven GM, Dynasty Architect, Legend and Immortal.
Rewind
Automatic snapshots taken at key decision points, listed under Rewind Timeline on the GM Profile screen. The game keeps up to ten. You can also take one yourself with Take Manual Snapshot. Restore returns the franchise to that week, and saves your current state first.
Challenge code
A code that recreates one exact league. A friend who pastes it on the start page and presses Accept Challenge starts the same league from the same point. This Week's Challenge starts the league everyone shares that week.
Commissioner mode
Up to four general managers sharing one league on the same device. There is no online multiplayer.

Contracts and the cap

Salary cap
The limit on what your club's contracts may add up to in a season. Every club in the league works under the same limit.
Cap Space
The room you have left under the cap. A signing, a trade or a franchise tag that does not fit is refused.
Cap hit
What one contract costs against this season's cap: the season's salary plus that season's share of the signing bonus.
Dead Cap
Cap room taken up by players who are no longer on your roster. When you release a player, the part of his signing bonus not yet counted lands on your cap at once, along with a share of any guaranteed money.
Restructure
Turns part of a player's salary for this season into bonus spread over the years left on his deal. This season's cap hit falls, later seasons cost more, and releasing him later leaves more dead cap. It needs more than one year left on the contract.
Franchise tag
Keeps one player whose contract is expiring for one more season on a fully guaranteed one-year deal, at a higher salary than he was on. Each club may use one tag a season, and it must fit under the cap.
Practice squad
Players held on separate weekly contracts outside the active roster. Elevate for Game makes one eligible for a single game, after which he returns to practice. Moving him To Active is a permanent promotion that needs a roster opening and cap room.
Injured Reserve (IR)
A designation for an injured player. It frees his place on the active roster while you keep paying his salary. You choose whether he can return this season or the placement ends his season.
Questionable
A game-time status of its own, separate from a player who is out for a stated number of weeks.

Scouting and the draft

Scouting fog
You see your scouts' estimate of a prospect's overall and potential, not the true figures. The potential of rival players and free agents is an estimate too. Your own players are shown exactly.
Scouting points
A weekly allowance, shown as Weekly Points in the Scouting Room. Spending points on a prospect moves your scouts' estimate closer to the truth.
Board
Your ranked list of up to 20 prospects. Add To Board puts a prospect on it, Up and Down reorder it, and Lock Board fixes it before the draft.

Your first season

A new franchise opens with the First Season Contract on the Desk. Its five objectives are a good order to learn the game in.

  1. Start. On the start page press Start Your Franchise for a league with a random club. To pick your club first, choose a team and press Create League.
  2. Read the Desk. It shows the calls waiting for you and the Front Office Advisor's argument for one of them. You can take the advice or ignore it.
  3. Set a depth chart yourself. Open Team and switch to Depth Chart. Drag a row to reorder a position group, or use Best Lineup and adjust from there. Undo takes you back.
  4. Sign a free agent. Open Free Agents and make an offer. The objective is credited once your offer wins and the player joins your club.
  5. Work the trade desk. Open Trades. Answer a rival's offer under Incoming Trade Offers, or build a deal of your own. Either counts.
  6. Play the weeks. Advance Week plays one week. Advance 4 Weeks and Advance Season move faster, and stop when something needs you.
  7. Reach the owner's win target. Finish the regular season at or above the number in the owner's mandate. The Desk shows the owner's confidence in you as the season goes on.
  8. Carry a legal cap into the offseason. Be under the cap when the season closes. The Contracts screen shows your Cap Space.

At the season review the contract closes with the owner's verdict on the objectives you met. If you get stuck, Open Game Guide inside the game has a how-to, the full rules and a list of actions.

Reading the salary cap

The Contracts screen is where you read and manage the cap. The Desk repeats your Cap Space and Dead Cap.

  1. Read the cards. Cap Space is the room you have left. Active Cap is what the players on your roster cost this season. Dead Cap is what you are still paying for players who have gone.
  2. Look ahead. The Cap War Room shows your cap over four years, so a deal that fits today does not surprise you later.
  3. Pick a player. Select a row in the Team Contract Board. The actions above it then apply to him.
  4. Keep a player. Re-sign and Negotiate work on a new deal; set the Years, and a Salary if you want to name one. Apply Tag uses your one franchise tag on a player whose contract is expiring. Exercise Option is for eligible players still on their rookie deal.
  5. Make room. Restructure lowers this season's cap hit by pushing cost into later seasons. Release, on the Team roster, removes a player's salary but leaves dead cap. Trade Player starts a trade for him, and his contract leaves with him.

A rule of thumb: a restructure is borrowing from your own future. It suits a club that is trying to win now, and it makes a rebuild harder later.

Drafting through scouting fog

You never see a prospect's true ratings before the draft. You see what your scouts think, and you decide how much to trust it.

  1. Open the Scouting Room. On the Scouting screen press Load Scouting Board. Weekly Points shows what you can spend this week.
  2. Spend points where the choice is close. Press Scout on a prospect's row. Each point spent moves the estimate of his overall and potential closer to the truth, and the table shows how confident your scouts are. Points come every week, so start early.
  3. Build your board. Add To Board puts a prospect on your board of up to 20. Up, Down and Remove put it in the order you want.
  4. Use the combine. In the offseason, the Draft Combine on the Draft screen adds measurements that narrow the uncertainty.
  5. Lock Board. Do this before the draft starts.
  6. Run the draft. In the Draft Room press Prepare Draft, then CPU Until User Pick to reach your turn. Select a player under Available Prospects and press Draft Selected Prospect. The draft has seven rounds. CPU Finish Draft completes the rest for you.

Rival general managers draft the way they build their clubs, and they may call during the draft to buy your pick. A prospect with a wide gap between his overall and his potential is a bet on development, not a sure thing.

Trading with rival general managers

All 31 rival clubs trade under the same roster, cap and deadline rules as you. Each rival general manager values players in his own way.

  1. Answer the phone. Rival offers appear on the Trades screen under Incoming Trade Offers, with what they want and why. Choose Accept, Counter at Trade Desk or Decline. An offer you do not answer lapses.
  2. Build your own deal. Choose the two clubs as Team A and Team B. Press Add on players in each club's Package Board and on picks under Draft Pick Assets.
  3. Evaluate before you offer. Evaluate Trade shows a Fairness figure and each club's cap change. The same player or pick cannot be offered twice.
  4. Execute Trade. The deal goes through only if the other club accepts and both clubs stay within the roster and cap rules.
  5. Know who you are dealing with. The League GM Archetypes table on the Scouting screen shows how each rival front office thinks. A club trying to win now accepts a wider price than one that is rebuilding. A rival pays more for a player at a position it needs and less at a position where it is already deep.
  6. Mind the deadline. There is one trade deadline in the season, and every club obeys it.

Rivals remember the deals you have done together, including an offer of theirs that you passed on. To put a player on your trade block, select him on the Contracts screen and press Toggle Trade Block.
